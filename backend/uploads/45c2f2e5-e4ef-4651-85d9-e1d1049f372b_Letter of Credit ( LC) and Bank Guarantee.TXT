Letters of Credit (LC): Ensuring Secure Payments in Global Trade
Posted On January 10, 2025

What Is a Letter of Credit (LC)? A Letter of Credit (LC) is a financial instrument issued by a bank or financial institution that guarantees payment to a seller or exporter in a trade transaction, provided the terms and conditions of the agreement are met. It is commonly used in international trade to reduce risk, offering assurance to both buyers and sellers.

Understanding How a Letter of Credit Works
A Letter of Credit acts as a safety net in trade transactions. Here is a simplified explanation of how it works:

Agreement Initiation: A buyer and seller enter into a contract, agreeing that payment will be made through an LC.

The buyer's bank (issuing bank) prepares the LC document and sends it to the seller's bank (advising bank).
The seller ships the goods and submits required documents, like the bill of lading and invoice, to the advising bank.
Verification and Payment: The advising bank checks the documents to ensure compliance with the LC terms. Upon approval, payment is made to the seller.
Why Is a Letter of Credit Required?
A Letter of Credit mitigates risks associated with trade, especially in cross-border transactions. It offers benefits such as:

Payment Security: Ensures the seller receives payment if the conditions of the LC are met.
Trust Building: Provides assurance to both buyers and sellers.
Risk Mitigation: Protects against buyer insolvency or non-payment.
Types of Letters of Credit
Different types of LCs serve various business needs. The main types include:
Revocable Letter of Credit: Can be altered or canceled by the issuing bank without prior notice to the seller.
Irrevocable Letter of Credit: Cannot be modified or canceled without agreement from all involved parties.
Confirmed Letter of Credit: Involves a second bank that guarantees payment, adding extra security.
Unconfirmed Letter of Credit: The payment guarantee is solely from the issuing bank.
Standby Letter of Credit: Acts as a backup, ensuring payment in case the buyer defaults.
Transferable Letter of Credit: Allows the beneficiary to transfer part or all of the credit to another party.
Back-to-Back Letter of Credit:Two separate LCs issued for the same transaction, often involving intermediaries.
