Question no: 4
Eval Question: what is FATCA (ACCOUNT OPENING FORM FOR RESIDENT INDIVIDUAL)
Eval Answer: This is a United States tax law. India signed an agreement with the US to help prevent tax evasion. Banks use this section to ask if you pay taxes in the US or any other foreign country. Question no: 6
Eval Question: normal/small/minor account type means what? (ACCOUNT OPENING FORM FOR RESIDENT INDIVIDUAL)
Eval Answer: Normal Account: A standard bank account. There are no strict upper limits on how much money you can hold or transfer, but you are usually required to maintain a minimum monthly balance (e.g., ₹1,000 to ₹3,000 depending on your branch location).  Minor Account: An account opened for a child under the age of 18. Because minors cannot legally enter contracts, a parent or legal guardian operates and oversees the account until the child turns 18.Small Account: A highly restricted, basic account meant for people who do not have officially valid KYC documents (like an Aadhaar or PAN card). You can open it with just a photograph and a signature. However, it has strict legal limits: you can never hold more than ₹50,000 in it, you cannot deposit more than ₹1 Lakh total in a single year, and you cannot withdraw more than ₹10,000 in a month.

Question no: 14
Eval Question: how to categorize asset as movable/immovable/liquid (Agriculture Loan Application Form)
Eval Answer: Immovable Assets: Things permanently attached to the earth that cannot be moved without destroying them. Examples: Your agricultural land, residential plots, your house, fixed farm sheds, or an open irrigation well. Movable Assets: Physical property that you own which can be easily shifted from one place to another. Examples: Tractors, harvesters, tillers, livestock/cattle, mobile pump sets, or harvested crops currently stored in your barn. Liquid Assets: Items that are already cash or can be converted into actual cash within 24 to 48 hours without losing their value. Examples: Cash in hand, balances in your savings accounts, Fixed Deposits (FDs), Gold jewelry, or National Savings Certificates (NSC).

Question no: 15
Eval Question: what is primary vs collateral security (Agriculture Loan Application Form)
Eval Answer: Primary Security: This is the direct object or asset being funded by the loan, or the immediate output of it. If you take a tractor loan, the tractor itself is the primary security. If you take a crop cultivation loan, the standing crops in your field serve as the primary security. If you default, the bank seizes this item first. Collateral Security: This is a backup guarantee (usually asked for larger loan amounts) in case the primary security isn't enough to cover the debt. For example, if you mortgage your ancestral land or family home as an extra guarantee for a tractor loan, that land or home is the collateral.

Eval Answer: Annuity Deposit: Think of this as the exact opposite of a loan EMI or a recurring deposit. You hand the bank a large lump sum of money all at once. Question no: 88
Eval Question: what is autosweeper or MOD (customer request form)
Eval Answer: Multi Option Deposit (MOD) Scheme feature turns your basic savings account into a dynamic wealth tool. It automatically links your savings account to a fixed deposit system to give you a higher interest rate without freezing your liquidity. You establish a threshold limit (for example, SBI triggers this at ₹50,000). Any extra cash sitting in your savings account that crosses this threshold is automatically "swept out" into a fixed deposit in multiples of ₹1,000 to earn higher interest rates. If you issue a check, pay a bill, or withdraw cash that exceeds the money left in your basic savings account, the system automatically triggers a "reverse sweep." It pulls the exact shortage amount back from your fixed deposit units (in multiples of ₹5,000) to clear the transaction automatically, protecting you from bounced checks or transaction failures.

Question no: 89
Eval Question: what is LIFO, FIFO (customer request form)
Eval Answer: When the system executes a "Reverse Sweep" to pull money back to cover a shortage in your savings account, it needs a rule to decide which fixed deposit unit to break if you have accumulated multiple ones over the year. The form allows you to choose between two tracking methods: LIFO (Last-In, First-Out): This is the bank's standard default choice. The system automatically breaks your most recent (newest) fixed deposit first. This method is generally preferred because it leaves your older fixed deposits untouched, allowing them to finish their maturity timelines and maximize their compounding interest yield. FIFO (First-In, First-Out): The system breaks your oldest fixed deposit first to cover the shortage. Choosing this means your newly created deposits are preserved, while the ones that have been running the longest are broken up prematurely.

Question no: 189
Eval Question: what does "reducing drawing power basis" mean (overdraft against salary form)
Eval Answer: Operating on a reducing drawing power basis means your maximum borrowing limit automatically shrinks in equal parts every month as you pay it back, rather than acting like a reusable credit card. For example, if you take a ₹60,000 overdraft for a 6-month timeline, your spending cap starts at ₹60,000. Question no: 190
Eval Question: what is MCLR (overdraft against salary form)
Eval Answer: MCLR stands for Marginal Cost of Funds Based Lending Rate. It is the internal benchmark floor interest rate below which a bank is legally not allowed to lend money to any customer. This rate is calculated automatically by the bank based on how much it costs them to maintain funds and pay interest on customer deposits. Because this overdraft uses a "6 months MCLR," your interest rate is tied to this baseline and will be reviewed and updated by the bank every six months to match prevailing economic market conditions.

Question no: 191
Eval Question: what is bps (overdraft against salary form)
Eval Answer: bps stands for Basis Points, which is a standard financial unit of measurement used by banks to describe minor changes or markups in interest rates. One single basis point is equal to exactly 0.01% (one-hundredth of a percentage point). In the context of this form, when the bank charges a rate "above the MCLR," they use bps as a clear, precise way to state their added profit margin without causing rounding confusion. For example, if the bank decides to charge you a premium margin of "250 bps" over a baseline MCLR of 8.50%, your total effective interest rate will be exactly 11.00%.

Question no: 201
Eval Question: difference in bullet repayment and emi schemes (gold loan application form)
Eval Answer: Under the Bullet Repayment scheme, a borrower does not pay any periodic installments to the bank during the tenure of the loan. Instead, the entire principal loan amount, along with all accumulated interest, is paid back in one single lump sum (the "bullet") at the end of the loan period (e.g., at 3, 6, or 12 months). Conversely, under an EMI-Based scheme, the loan is paid off steadily over a fixed schedule through monthly installments comprising both a portion of the principal amount and the accrued interest, which progressively reduces the total outstanding debt balance until the end of the tenure.
